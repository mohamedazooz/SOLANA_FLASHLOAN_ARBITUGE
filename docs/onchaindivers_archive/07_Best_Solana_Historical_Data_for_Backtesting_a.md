y1="3" y2="10"></line><line x1="3" x2="10" y1="21" y2="14"></line></svg></button></div></div></div></a></figure></div><p>Data is the part of a Solana backtest that takes the longest and gets the least attention. You need every trade, not candles; bonding-curve launches, not only migrated pools; and the fees and reserves that turn a signal into a realistic result. Few sources give you all of that for a full year, and a year of PumpSwap trades alone is over five billion rows.</p><p>This article covers what to look for in historical data, the forms it comes in, seven providers worth trying and what to check during a trial.</p><h2>What do you need from historical data?</h2><p>For backtesting:</p><ul><li><p><strong>Enough history, and knowing whether it stays.</strong> Data deep enough to cover your test period on the venues you trade. Some sources keep fixed history, others a rolling window, and it is worth knowing which before you build a test you plan to rerun.</p></li><li><p><strong>Individual trades.</strong> Candles hide who traded, in what order and at what size, which is where most Solana strategies live.</p></li><li><p><strong>Execution context.</strong> Pool reserves before and after each trade, fees, and the priority fee paid to land it. Without them, slippage and costs are guesses.</p></li><li><p><strong>Bonding-curve coverage.</strong> Most Pump.fun tokens never migrate to an AMM, so pool-only data misses most launches.</p></li></ul><p>For machine learning, on top of that:</p><ul><li><p><strong>Large exports.</strong> Training sets run to tens or hundreds of millions of rows, so pricing by request, credit or data point quickly becomes the limit.</p></li><li><p><strong>Raw rows.</strong> Features like order-flow imbalance or the share of flow from a given router are built from individual trades.</p></li><li><p><strong>Execution fields as features.</strong> Priority fees, compute units and the calling program often say more about who is trading than price does.</p></li><li><p><strong>A stable schema.</strong> If a column changes meaning halfway through your training window, features break silently.</p></li></ul><div><hr></div><h2>Three forms Solana data comes in</h2><p><strong>Raw chain data.</strong> Transactions exactly as the chain recorded them, through RPC or streams. Everything is there, and turning instructions into trades is your job.</p><p><strong>Decoded trades you query yourself.</strong> Swaps already decoded into rows, in a database you query with SQL or delivered into your own warehouse. You compute whatever metrics you need from individual trades.</p><p><strong>Data through an API.</strong> Endpoints for prices, candles, trades and wallets. Quick to start with, and you work with the fields and formats each endpoint returns.</p><p>For backtesting and ML, decoded trades at the level of individual swaps are usually the most practical starting point. Raw data takes the most work to use, and API endpoints can make large exports slow and expensive.</p><div><hr></div><h2>Ready-made options</h2><p>There is no single price for a 1-year+ of Solana data. What you pay depends on the form you take it in and how much you pull. Most APIs and query platforms charge by credits or API points, so cost grows with the volume you query and export. Raw chain data costs less to access, but storage, decoding and backfill are yours to run. Decoded trades in a database are usually a flat fee.</p><p><strong>The prices below are starting points, not the cost of a specific workload. Starting prices as listed on each provider’s public website in October 2026. Several providers discount annual billing, and plans change often: check the provider’s own site for current pricing and limits.</em></p><h3>Onchain Divers</h3><p>Real-time and historical decoded Solana trades with direct SQL access to the database (ClickHouse), not an API. </p><ul><li><p><strong>Pricing:</strong> flat monthly fee, $200/month</p></li><li><p><strong>Free trial:</strong> 1 week, full database access with no query limits</p></li><li><p><strong>Best for:</strong> trade-level backtests and ML on Pump.fun, Pumpswap, Raydium and other main Solana venues</p></li></ul><h3>Dune</h3><p>Decoded tables you query with SQL inside Dune, plus an API. </p><ul><li><p><strong>Pricing:</strong> credits, from $75/month (4,000 credits); $399/month for 25,000 credits; CSV export from $399</p></li><li><p><strong>Free plan:</strong> view-only</p></li><li><p><strong>Best for:</strong> exploration, dashboards and shared analysis</p></li></ul><h3>GoldRush</h3><p>Decoded data through an API; delivery to your own warehouse is priced separately.</p><ul><li><p><strong>Pricing:</strong> API credits, from $10/month (10,000 credits); $250/month for 300,000 credits</p></li><li><p><strong>Free trial:</strong> 14 days</p></li><li><p><strong>Best for:</strong> multi-chain projects and data in your own warehouse</p></li></ul><h3>Bitquery</h3><p>A multi-chain GraphQL API, with historical price and OHLCV series for Solana sold as an archive add-on.</p><ul><li><p><strong>Pricing:</strong> API points, from $49/month; the Solana historical archive add-on starts at $300/month</p></li><li><p><strong>Free trial:</strong> 7 days</p></li><li><p><strong>Best for:</strong> multi-chain projects and historical price and OHLCV series</p></li></ul><h3>Birdeye</h3><p>A REST API for Solana market data.</p><ul><li><p><strong>Pricing:</strong> plans with request limits, from $99/month</p></li><li><p><strong>Free plan:</strong> yes</p></li><li><p><strong>Best for:</strong> candles, token metrics and wallet lookups</p></li></ul><h3>Shrine API</h3><p>Real-time Solana trades, candles and launch streams, plus a trading API.</p><ul><li><p><strong>Pricing:</strong> free, 0.1 SOL/month or 1 SOL/month, with higher limits and faster streams on paid plans</p></li><li><p><strong>Free plan:</strong> yes</p></li><li><p><strong>Best for:</strong> live trades, 1-second candles, new launches and migrations.</p></li></ul><h3>Helius</h3><p>Raw chain data through RPC and streams. Helius sits apart from the rest: it is infrastructure rather than a dataset. It gives you fast access to the raw chain, and turning that into a history of trades is your pipeline’s job.</p><ul><li><p><strong>Pricing:</strong> credits by plan: $49, $499 or $999/month</p></li><li><p><strong>Free plan:</strong> yes</p></li><li><p><strong>Best for:</strong> building your own pipeline</p></li></ul><div><hr></div><h2>What to check during a trial</h2><p>A week of real queries against real data tells you more than any comparison table, including this one. For the venues you care about, check:</p><ul><li><p><strong>History depth.</strong> How far back trade-level data goes, and whether that history is fixed or rolling.</p></li><li><p><strong>Bonding curves.</strong> Whether Pump.fun bonding-curve trades are included, not only migrated pools.</p></li><li><p><strong>Cost fields.</strong> Whether you get pool reserves, fees and priority fees, so slippage and costs are measured rather than guessed.</p></li><li><p><strong>Export.</strong> How long it takes to get a year of data into your own environment, and what limits that: credits, rate limits, row caps or query timeouts.</p></li><li><p><strong>Schema.</strong> Whether field meanings are documented and stay stable across the period you train on.</p></li></ul><div><hr></div><h2>What about building your own indexer?</h2><p><strong>You can:</strong> the chain is public and archive RPC exists. In practice it means a separate decoder for every venue, from Pump.fun and PumpSwap to Raydium and Meteora, kept working as programs upgrade; weeks of backfill and about 1.9 TB of compressed storage for a single year of PumpSwap alone; and gaps and quirks that fail silently. </p><p>Your own indexer makes sense when you need venues or history nobody offers, or full control over every field. Otherwise the time usually goes further on the research itself.</p><div><hr></div><h2>The short version</h2><p>For backtesting and ML on Solana, the headline price is rarely what decides it. What decides it is whether you get individual trades with the context to price them, history that covers your test period, and a way to pull it at the scale you work at. None of that is visible on a pricing page, and all of it shows up within a few days of real queries. Pick two or three options that fit your task, run your own queries in their trials, and let the data decide.</p><div><hr></div><h3>Sources</h3><ul><li><p><a href="https://onchaindivers.com">OnchainDivers</a></p></li><li><p><a href="https://dune.com/pricing">Dune pricing</a></p></li><li><p><a href="https://docs.dune.com/resources/credits-billing/how-credits-work">Dune: how credits work</a></p></li><li><p><a href="https://goldrush.dev/pricing">GoldRush pricing</a></p></li><li><p><a href="https://goldrush.dev/docs/goldrush-solana/warehouse/dex-swaps">GoldRush Solana warehouse: DEX swaps</a></p></li><li><p><a href="https://bitquery.io/pricing">Bitquery pricing</a></p></li><li><p><a href="https://birdeye.so/data-api/pricing">Birdeye Data API pricing</a></p></li><li><p><a href="https://shrine.trade/api/">Shrine API</a></p></li><li><p><a href="https://www.helius.dev/pricing">Helius pricing</a></p></li></ul>
