Someone has submitted a logo. An analytics team has mapped its contracts, indexed its trades and built a dashboard. At that point discovery becomes navigation: pick a known project and inspect its metrics.</p><p><strong>Wassup starts one step earlier.</strong></p><p>It asks a less comfortable question: <em>what code is active on-chain right now, even if nobody has labeled it yet?</em></p><p>The app now tracks both <a href="https://wassup.trenchscreener.ai/?chain=solana&amp;sort=dailyTxCount&amp;order=desc">Solana<span>(opens in new tab)</span></a> and <a href="https://wassup.trenchscreener.ai/?chain=robinhood&amp;sort=dailyTxCount&amp;order=desc">Robinhood Chain<span>(opens in new tab)</span></a>. On Solana it samples program calls. On Robinhood Chain it samples contracts emitting logs. It records where each address appeared, when it was first and last observed, and how frequently it appeared in the current 24-hour sample.[1][2]</p><p>This is deliberately not a complete indexer. Wassup is a <strong>probabilistic activity map</strong>: cheap enough to maintain a broad radar, but informative enough to tell a researcher where a full-history investigation may be worth the cost.[1][2]</p><p>The leaderboard is not the answer. It is the beginning of the query.</p><h2><strong>What Wassup measures—and what it does not</strong></h2><p>The most important field is also the easiest to misuse: the sampled 24-hour activity count.</p><p>A high value means an address repeatedly appeared in Wassup’s current sample. It does <strong>not</strong> automatically mean:</p><ul><li><p>that many users;</p></li><li><p>that many unique transactions;</p></li><li><p>that many swaps;</p></li><li><p>organic demand;</p></li><li><p>volume, fees, TVL or revenue;</p></li><li><p>a good or safe protocol.</p></li></ul><p>On Solana, one transaction can invoke several programs through cross-program invocations. A router may call an AMM, which calls token programs, while Compute Budget and account-management instructions appear beside them.[3][4] On an EVM chain, one user transaction can cause several contracts to emit logs, and every pool in a factory family can occupy its own leaderboard row.</p><p>Wassup therefore measures <strong>visibility in a sample</strong>, not economic importance.</p><p>That distinction is the product thesis. A complete index is expensive to build for every anonymous program and contract. A probabilistic map lets us reserve expensive decoding, trace reconstruction and full-history queries for the small residual that looks genuinely unusual.</p><h2><strong>The workflow: leaderboard → residual → attribution</strong></h2><p>In practice, I use Wassup in five passes.</p><h3><strong>1. Freeze the observation</strong></h3><p>The ranking is live, so counts move while you investigate. Save the UTC observation time, ranking parameters and rows before making numerical claims.[1][2]</p><p><code>first seen</code> also means first observed by Wassup—not necessarily deployment time.[1][2] A binary may exist quietly for days before a frontend opens, an aggregator integrates it or bots discover it.</p><h3><strong>2. Remove universal infrastructure</strong></h3><p>On Solana, the first rows are usually dominated by runtime and token plumbing: Compute Budget, System Program, SPL Token, Associated Token and Token-2022.[1] On Robinhood Chain, the equivalent noise comes from known routers and large families of pool contracts.[2]</p><p>Removing these rows is not cosmetic. In the Solana snapshot used for this research, the first four infrastructure programs represented 64.8% of all positive sampled hits in the inspected ranking, and the first seven represented 85.8%.[1]</p><p>The useful list begins after subtraction.</p><h3><strong>3. Rank recent concentration, not only raw activity</strong></h3><p>A simple queueing heuristic is:</p><div class="highlighted_code_block" data-attrs="{&quot;language&quot;:&quot;plaintext&quot;,&quot;nodeId&quot;:&quot;05d93ed5-e4b6-40c8-bdee-8e1c089720d8&quot;}" data-component-name="HighlightedCodeBlockToDOM"><pre class="shiki"><code class="language-plaintext">recent concentration = sampled_hits_24h / sampled_hits_all_time
research score = sampled_hits_24h × recent concentration</code></pre></div><p>This favors programs that are both active and unusually concentrated in the current window. It does not prove that they are new. It asks a narrower question: <em><strong>which addresses look most different from their own sampled history?</strong></em></p><p>For Robinhood Chain, the more useful operation is often family collapse. In the inspected pages, repeated <code>Swap</code>, <code>Mint</code>, <code>Burn</code> and <code>Collect</code> signatures showed why related pools should become one protocol-family row before the remaining addresses are ranked.[2]</p><h3><strong>4. Reconstruct behavior</strong></h3><p>This is where the two chains diverge.</p><p><strong>On Solana</strong>, inspect the program and CPI layers separately:[3][4]</p><ul><li><p>top-level calls versus CPI;</p></li><li><p>instruction names or discriminators;</p></li><li><p>token and SOL balance changes;</p></li><li><p>ProgramData and upgrade authority;</p></li><li><p>repeated low-compute state updates versus token-moving routes;</p></li><li><p>integration registries such as Jupiter’s program-label map.[5]</p></li></ul><p><strong>On Robinhood Chain</strong>, inspect:</p><ul><li><p>event <code>topic0</code> signatures;</p></li><li><p>verified source and ABI;</p></li><li><p>proxy implementation slots;</p></li><li><p>constructor and deployer links;</p></li><li><p>clusters of core, vault, router and reward contracts;</p></li><li><p>whether events represent economic actions or housekeeping.</p></li></ul><p>The goal is not merely to assign a name. It is to determine what role the address plays inside a system.</p><h3><strong>5. Keep attribution confidence separate from functional confidence</strong></h3><p>Sometimes the function is obvious while ownership is not.</p><p>A Solana binary can clearly initialize binary markets, split outcome tokens and redeem winners without publishing a branded address list.[8][9]</p><p>An EVM proxy can clearly rebalance Uniswap V3 positions while the frontend relationship remains indirect.[13][14]</p><p>Wassup should be able to say:</p><ul><li><p><strong>confirmed identity</strong>;</p></li><li><p><strong>high-confidence function, operator unknown</strong>;</p></li><li><p><strong>candidate attribution</strong>;</p></li><li><p><strong>unknown</strong>.</p></li></ul><p>Forcing every active address into a familiar brand would make the database look cleaner and the research worse.</p><h2><strong>A few fun things the radar found</strong></h2><p>The findings are useful demonstrations, but they are not the main product. What matters is that the same lightweight interface produced leads on two very different execution models.</p><h3><strong>Solana: a product layer hiding behind an unlabeled program</strong></h3><p>One of the strongest Solana residuals was:</p><div class="highlighted_code_block" data-attrs="{&quot;language&quot;:&quot;plaintext&quot;,&quot;nodeId&quot;:&quot;363718a0-73b4-4e09-9de4-19cbe525d76f&quot;}" data-component-name="HighlightedCodeBlockToDOM"><pre class="shiki"><code class="language-plaintext">6Vo3245eszAb5wuqEMw8mGdbfRUdKbHhDHP5LcaGuTAB</code></pre></div><p>Wassup first observed it on August 15. In the frozen snapshot it had 26,936 sampled 24-hour hits against 28,167 sampled all-time hits.[1]</p><p>The row had no useful name, IDL or Jupiter label. Its observed instructions did:[1][6]</p><ul><li><p><code>BondingCurveV3</code></p></li><li><p><code>PumpSwapV3</code></p></li><li><p><code>SellPumpSwapPercentage</code></p></li></ul><p>Transactions showed it routing across active Pump bonding curves and migrated PumpSwap pools.[6] A DefiLlama adapter review attributes the exact address to trades placed through Pump.fun’s official mobile app.[6]</p><p>A neighboring program gave us an additional cross-check. Pump.fun’s official IDL names <code>MAyh…MD4e</code> as <code>mayhem_program_id</code>.[7] The two programs share the same live upgrade authority. Together, the evidence resolves the mystery address as Pump.fun’s mobile trade-router layer rather than an unrelated trading bot.[6][7]</p><p>That is a good Wassup result: not “Pump.fun is active,” which everyone knows, but “this previously unlabeled program is a distinct product-specific execution surface.”</p><h3><strong>Solana: prediction infrastructure appeared before the address directory</strong></h3><p>Another program, <code>prediCt…6tM</code>, exposed instructions including <code>InitializeMarket</code>, <code>Split</code>, <code>Merge</code>, <code>DetermineOutcome</code> and <code>RedeemOutcome</code>.[9]</p><p>A live DFlow-routed transaction invokes it to split and mint binary outcome tokens, then creates and updates user settings.[9] DFlow’s official product announcement describes a tokenization layer bringing Kalshi prediction markets to Solana.[8]</p><p>DFlow’s public pages did not print the exact program address, so the careful label is <strong>DFlow-compatible native prediction program</strong>, with strong functional evidence and cautious ownership attribution.</p><p>The same snapshot also surfaced <code>BisonFi Predict</code>, independently labeled by Jupiter, and GatorSwap already appearing inside aggregator routes.[1][5]</p><p>Those are fun clues, not declarations of adoption. They show that integration activity can become visible before documentation and dashboards catch up.[1][5]</p><h3><strong>Robinhood Chain: one leaderboard band resolved into a lending stack</strong></h3><p>On Robinhood Chain, pages six and seven were more useful than page one. After collapsing repeated Uniswap-style pools, one address stood out through a lending grammar: <code>Supply</code>, <code>SupplyCollateral</code>, <code>Borrow</code>, <code>IncrementNonce</code> and <code>SetAuthorization</code>.</p><p>The address, <code>0x9D53…1010</code>, is the official Robinhood Chain deployment of the Morpho core contract.[10][11]</p><p>A nearby address, <code>0xBeEff…09dd</code>, resolved into the <strong>Steakhouse USDG</strong> Morpho Vault V2 deployment.[12]</p><p>The fun part was not discovering that Morpho exists. It was seeing the core lending layer and a curated allocation vault appear independently in the same sampled activity band. Wassup turned two rows into a system-level follow-up: which markets receive capital, how concentrated are depositors, and how much activity comes from allocators or keepers?</p><h3><strong>Robinhood Chain: an LP manager hiding among pool traffic</strong></h3><p><code>0x1195…6DCE</code> initially looked like more Uniswap-related noise. Resolving its EIP-1967 implementation led to <code>SnuggleVaultUpgradeable</code>, a concentrated-liquidity position manager with deposits, keeper actions, fee harvesting and rebalancing.[13][14]</p><p>DefiLlama’s deployment table identifies the instance as <strong>MaxFi Robinhood</strong> and describes MaxFi as a Snuggle whitelabel.[15]</p><p>This is exactly why event-family collapse matters. The address is downstream of Uniswap V3, but it is not another pool. It is an automation layer managing LP positions.</p><h3><strong>Robinhood Chain: randomness led to a small application graph</strong></h3><p>An unverified contract at <code>0x49d5…Ef80</code> repeatedly emitted <code>RandomnessRequested</code>. The official SLVR application maps it to <code>swapLottery</code>, while SLVR describes its product as an on-chain Grid Mining system.[16][17][18]</p><p>A related verified contract, <code>0xaF685…7200</code>, implements vote-escrow staking and emits reward-distribution and claim events.[19]</p><p>Following one anonymous event therefore revealed a graph: lottery rounds, randomness, vote-escrow positions and reward accounting. That still says nothing about randomness quality, user diversity or sustainable economics. It says the system is coherent enough to investigate.</p><h2><strong>Why probabilistic discovery is useful</strong></h2><p>There are two bad extremes in on-chain research.</p><p>At one extreme, index everything. Decode every anonymous binary, retain every trace and classify every contract. The result is comprehensive, expensive and frequently wasted on addresses nobody will ever inspect.</p><p>At the other extreme, index only known projects. The result is efficient, polished and late.</p><p>Wassup sits between them:</p><ol><li><p>sample broadly;</p></li><li><p>rank cheaply;</p></li><li><p>subtract known infrastructure;</p></li><li><p>decode the residual;</p></li><li><p>escalate the best leads to full-history analysis;</p></li><li><p>feed verified names and links back into the app.</p></li></ol><p>The loop improves the tool. A mystery address becomes a named program; related contracts become a family; the next unexplained residual becomes easier to see.</p><p>This also makes negative results valuable. A program that produces thousands of calls may turn out to be a token-account cleaner. A high-frequency venue may combine real swaps with tiny quote-state updates. A randomness request may come from one automated operator. Wassup has still done its job: it directed attention, and the investigation prevented the counter from becoming a misleading business metric.</p><h2><strong>A minimal reproducible first pass</strong></h2><p>The app is server-rendered, so the starting queue can be reproduced with a small script:</p><div class="highlighted_code_block" data-attrs="{&quot;language&quot;:&quot;python&quot;,&quot;nodeId&quot;:&quot;df4bda26-2be5-429f-a6c2-a27863f14df4&quot;}" data-component-name="HighlightedCodeBlockToDOM"><pre class="shiki"><code class="language-python">import requests
from bs4 import BeautifulSoup

rows = []
for chain, pages in {"solana": range(1, 5), "robinhood": (6, 7)}.items():
    for page in pages:
        html = requests.get(
            "https://wassup.trenchscreener.ai/",
            params={
                "page": page,
                "chain": chain,
                "sort": "dailyTxCount",
                "order": "desc",
            },
            timeout=30,
        ).text
        soup = BeautifulSoup(html, "html.parser")
        for tr in soup.select("tbody tr"):
            td = tr.select("td")
            if not td:
                continue
            rows.append({
                "chain": chain,
                "address": td[1].get_text(strip=True),
                "name": td[2].get_text(" ", strip=True),
                "sampled_all_time": int(td[5].get_text(strip=True)),
                "sampled_24h": int(td[6].get_text(strip=True)),
            })</code></pre></div><p>The script is not the research. It creates the queue. The research begins when program calls, CPI, event grammar, proxies, authorities and official deployments are used to explain why an address is there.</p><h2><strong>The product in one sentence</strong></h2><p>Wassup is not trying to tell you which protocol is biggest.</p><p>It is trying to show you <strong>which active piece of code deserves a closer look before somebody else has already named it, indexed it and placed it on page one</strong>.</p><p>The leaderboard is the interface.
