# The 17.6 SOL Ghost: Reverse-Engineering Pump.fun's BOOST Migration Arb

- **Date**: 2026-07-24
- **Original URL**: [https://onchaindivers.substack.com/p/the-176-sol-ghost-reverse-engineering](https://onchaindivers.substack.com/p/the-176-sol-ghost-reverse-engineering)

<p>It started as a monitoring anomaly. On July 22 our pump.fun indexer and one of our RPC nodes both started sweating: a burst of <code>getProgramAccounts</code> calls hammering the bonding-curve program, and a wave of <em>old</em> tokens — some launched back in early 2025 — suddenly migrating to the AMM. Dead coins don’t migrate. Dead coins sit at 60 SOL on a curve nobody will finish for months.</p><p>Except that day, ~230 of them completed in 24 hours, up from the usual 2–6.</p><div class="subscription-widget-wrap-editor" data-attrs="{&quot;url&quot;:&quot;https://onchaindivers.substack.com/subscribe?&quot;,&quot;text&quot;:&quot;Subscribe&quot;,&quot;language&quot;:&quot;en&quot;}" data-component-name="SubscribeWidgetToDOM"><div class="subscription-widget show-subscribe"><div class="preamble"><p class="cta-caption">Thanks for reading! This post is us reverse-engineering it from chain state: what pump.fun’s new <strong>BOOST</strong> feature actually does to virtual liquidity, where the free 17.6 SOL comes from, the exact formulas, the indexer queries we used to hunt targets, and a profitability table by curve size. Then the honest part — why the theoretical +15 SOL is really +2 to +4, and how you get stuck.</p><h2><strong>What BOOST is</strong></h2><p>Pump.fun shipped <strong>BOOST</strong> on 2026-07-21 (10:23 EST). The mechanic, from their own announcement: for <strong>~5 minutes after every migration</strong>, the protocol runs a <strong>TWAP buyback-and-burn</strong> into the freshly created pool — roughly <strong>17.6 SOL</strong> for SOL-paired tokens (≈ $2,516 for USDC pairs). They describe it as recycling “dead liquidity” — about 20% of a migrated pool’s value that, mathematically, can never be traded back out. Tokens migrated <em>before</em> the cutoff, or launched via Mayhem, get <strong>no</strong> boost.</p><p>That “dead liquidity” line is the whole story. Let’s find the 17.6 SOL.</p><h2><strong>The bonding curve, in numbers</strong></h2><p>A standard pump.fun curve is a constant-product AMM with <strong>virtual reserves</strong>. State lives in the <code>BondingCurve</code> account:</p><pre><code><code>offset  0  discriminator            8 bytes  (sha256("account:BondingCurve")[:8])
offset  8  virtual_token_reserves   u64 LE
offset 16  virtual_sol_reserves     u64 LE
offset 24  real_token_reserves      u64 LE
offset 32  real_sol_reserves        u64 LE   &lt;- "S": how full the curve is
offset 40  token_total_supply       u64 LE
offset 48  complete                 u8
offset 49  creator                  pubkey 32 bytes</code></code></pre><p>The invariant is the usual one:</p><pre><code><code>k = virtual_sol_reserves × virtual_token_reserves   (constant)</code></code></pre><p>For the standard curve the launch parameters are:</p><pre><code><code>virtual_sol_reserves₀   = 30 SOL          (pure virtual, no real SOL backing it)
virtual_token_reserves₀ = 1,073 M tokens
k                       = 30 × 1073 ≈ 32,190   (M·SOL)</code></code></pre><p>Price at any point is <code>vsol / vtok</code>, so the opening price is <code>30 / 1073 = 0.028 SOL/M</code>. As people buy, real SOL flows in, <code>vsol</code> rises, <code>vtok</code> falls along the hyperbola. The curve <strong>completes</strong> when real SOL hits <strong>85</strong> — i.e. <code>vsol = 30 + 85 = 115</code>:</p><pre><code><code>vtok_at_completion = k / vsol = 32,190 / 115 = 279.9 M tokens</code></code></pre><p>So of the 1,073 M nominal token side, <code>1073 − 279.9 = 793 M</code> get sold into circulation during the bonding phase. The remaining <strong>~206.9 M</strong> tokens are what pump deposits into the new AMM pool.</p><h2><strong>Finding the ghost: the 17.6 SOL of “dead liquidity”</strong></h2><p>Here’s the part everyone glosses over. When the curve migrates, pump seeds the AMM pool with those <strong>206.9 M tokens</strong>, but it has to open the pool at the <em>same price</em> the curve ended on so there’s no instant gap to arb. The curve’s final price was:</p><pre><code><code>P_final = 115 / 279.9 = 0.411 SOL/M</code></code></pre><p>To make a pool of 206.9 M tokens quote at 0.411 SOL/M, the SOL side must be worth:</p><pre><code><code>quote_needed = 206.9 M × 0.411 = 85 SOL</code></code></pre><p><strong>This is where pump changed something — and where the marketing starts.</strong> Until 2026-07-21 the migration handed the pool the <em>full</em> real SOL. Read a pre-cutoff pool from our indexer and the numbers are clean:</p><pre><code><code>OLD regime (migrated before 2026-07-21):
pool_base_token_reserves   = 206.90 M tokens
pool_quote_token_reserves  =  84.99 SOL      (all REAL)
virtual_quote_reserves     =   0.00 SOL      (didn't exist)</code></code></pre><p><span>Now read the very first swap of a </span><em>post-cutoff</em><span> pool (</span><code>pumpswap_all_swaps</code><span>, token </span><code>EkLNaDq…</code><span>):</span></p><pre><code><code>NEW regime (BOOST):
pool_base_token_reserves   = 206.90 M tokens
pool_quote_token_reserves  =  67.41 SOL      (REAL sol in the pool)
virtual_quote_reserves     =  17.58 SOL      (phantom — Int128 field, constant for life)
--------------------------------------------------------------------
effective quote            =  84.99 SOL   -&gt;  price = 84.99 / 206.9 = 0.411 SOL/M  ✓</code></code></pre><p><span>Same price, same token side. The only change: pump </span><strong>carved 17.58 real SOL out of the pool and replaced it with a virtual quote reserve</strong><span> — an entry that props the quoted price up but can never be withdrawn. The curve still raised ~85 (</span><code>pfamm_migrations.sol_amount = 84.99</code><span>) and the migration fee is a rounding error (</span><code>pool_migration_fee = 0.015 SOL</code><span>); the withheld 17.58 gets drip-fed back over the next 5 minutes as the BOOST buyback. Same 17.58, three ways, all measured on-chain:</span></p><pre><code><code>carved out     = old_real − new_real = 84.99 − 67.41 ≈ 17.58 SOL   (what BOOST spends)
virtual_quote_reserves (read off the new pool)      = 17.58 SOL     (where it's now booked)
residual       = pooled_tokens × P_final − new_real = 17.58 SOL     ("dead liquidity")</code></code></pre><p>Here’s the honest part pump’s announcement skips: <strong>an AMM pool is just another bonding curve</strong> — same constant-product shape, different parameters. And that ~17.58 SOL was <em>never</em> extractable, in either regime. A constant-product pool holding 206.9 M tokens can never pay out its whole SOL side, because price → the floor as the circulating 793 M floods in (proof below). In the old regime that ~17.58 sat locked in the pool as genuinely stuck <em>real</em> SOL. Pump didn’t <em>fix</em> dead liquidity — it <strong>relabeled the stuck real SOL as virtual, pocketed the freed 17.58, and re-spends it as a buyback</strong> that arbers and snipers farm. Calling that “reinjecting dead liquidity” is marketing; the dead liquidity is still dead. For 5 minutes there’s simply now a price-insensitive buyer with 17.6 SOL — and that’s the whole edge.</p><h3><strong>Proof the migration does </strong><em><strong>not</strong></em><strong> preserve k</strong></h3><p>Here’s the structural flaw the whole edge rests on. To <em>actually</em> continue the curve — same price <strong>and</strong> same liquidity depth — the pool would need the curve’s ending reserves: <code>vsol = 115, vtok = 279.9 M</code>, i.e. <code>k = 32,190</code>. It doesn’t get them. The pool’s effective reserves are <code>85 × 206.9</code>:</p><pre><code><code>k_curve = 115   × 279.9 = 32,190
k_pool  = 84.99 × 206.9 = 17,585      -&gt;  k_pool / k_curve = 0.546</code></code></pre><p><strong>The pool is only 55% as deep as the curve it replaced</strong>, at the <em>same</em> price. Price is preserved; <code>k</code> is not. Both sides are scaled down by √0.546 ≈ 0.74 (quote 85 vs 115, base 206.9 vs 279.9). Note this was already true in the <strong>old</strong> regime — <code>84.99 × 206.9</code> is the same <code>k_pool</code> — so the shallowness, and the dead ~17.58, predate BOOST entirely. That’s exactly why the ~793 M tokens already in circulation can never come home: the pool holds only 206.9 M tokens against them. BOOST didn’t change the depth or the trapped SOL; it just changed whether that trapped 17.58 is <em>real and locked</em> (old) or <em>virtual and re-spent as a buyback</em> (new).</p><h2><strong>The arbitrage</strong></h2><p>If a fresh pool comes with a guaranteed ~17.6 SOL buyer, the play writes itself:</p><ol><li><p>Find a <strong>stalled</strong> curve already holding a decent chunk of real SOL <code>S</code>.</p></li><li><p><strong>Complete it</strong> yourself — buy the last <code>85 − S</code> SOL worth of the curve. This sweeps a bag of <code>bag_M</code> tokens <em>and</em> triggers migration.</p></li><li><p>Migration seeds the pool and BOOST starts buying.</p></li><li><p><strong>Dump your bag</strong> into the boosted pool. The TWAP buyback (plus organic snipers) absorbs it.</p></li></ol><p>You captured the boost, minus your completion cost, minus slippage and fees. The completion cost <code>85 − S</code> is smaller the fuller the curve already is — so old, half-finished curves that stalled at 50–70 SOL are ideal: cheap to finish, and the bag you sweep is small enough to sell without cratering the pool.</p><h3><strong>The formulas</strong></h3><p>Given a curve’s on-chain state <code>(vsol, vtok, S=real_sol_reserves)</code>:</p><pre><code><code>cost      = 85 − S                                  # SOL to finish the curve
vsol_end  = vsol + cost
bag_M     = vtok − k / vsol_end                     # tokens you sweep en route to completion
dump      = 85 × bag_M / (206.9 + bag_M)            # SOL back from selling bag into the pool
boost     = min(17.6, dump)                         # boost you actually soak up
net       = dump + boost − cost − fees</code></code></pre><p>The <code>dump</code> term is just the constant-product output of selling <code>bag_M</code> tokens into a pool of <code>(206.9 M tokens, 85 eff-SOL)</code>. Note the two-sided tension baked in:</p><ul><li><p><strong>Emptier curve (low S)</strong> → cheap-ish to finish but you sweep a <em>huge</em> bag; selling it moves the pool hard, so slippage eats you.</p></li><li><p><strong>Fuller curve (high S)</strong> → tiny bag, low slippage, but the bag is too small to soak up the full 17.6 SOL boost.</p></li></ul><p>The optimum is where your bag’s sale value just <strong>saturates</strong> the 17.6 boost with minimal slippage.</p><h3><strong>What you’re actually arbing: a double-counted 17.6 SOL</strong></h3><p>Strip away the mechanics and the edge is an accounting overlap. <strong>The same 17.58 SOL is booked twice</strong>, and the arb front-runs everyone else to collect both copies:</p><ol><li><p><strong>As virtual quote reserves (17.58, phantom).</strong> It isn’t in the pool — it’s a number that holds the quoted price at 0.411 instead of the real-backed <code>67.41 / 206.9 = 0.326</code>. It inflates the <em>depth</em> you sell against.</p></li><li><p><strong>As the real BOOST buyback (17.58, cash).</strong> The identical 17.58 that was withheld from the raise is drip-fed back in as a live, price-insensitive buyer.</p></li></ol><p>During the 5-minute window the pool therefore <em>presents</em> ~85 effective quote <strong>plus</strong> an incoming ~17.58 of real buys — call it ~102 of apparent liquidity — while only ~85 real SOL will ever exist for it (67.41 deposited + 17.58 returned). That extra 17.58 is pure phantom. Whoever <strong>completes the curve and dumps into the boost window</strong> sells at prices held up by the virtual copy <em>and</em> gets paid in real SOL by the buyback copy — banking the 17.58 twice over before the virtual is exposed as empty.</p><p>Who eats it? The pre-migration bagholders. The virtual 17.58 never converts to cash; once the real 67.41 is drained and the boost is skimmed, late sellers hit the virtual floor and get crumbs (quantified in “what happens if everyone sells”, below). The arb isn’t creating SOL from nothing — the books balance at ~85 real — it’s <strong>timing the overlap</strong> to capture the returning 17.58 before the holders it was nominally protecting can touch it.</p><h2><strong>Profitability by curve size</strong></h2><p>Running the model across the whole range of <code>S</code> (fee = 1 SOL for priority + tip + curve fees):</p><div That works, but pump has <strong>&gt;10 M accounts</strong>, sweet-spot curves are <strong>rarer than 1 in 15,000</strong>, and V2 paginates over <em>storage</em> (a reserve memcmp just yields empty pages) — so you walk millions of accounts per scan. That’s the load we saw others generating. Ground-truth, but brutal on an RPC.</p><p>The fast path is an indexer. Every swap we store carries <code>virtual_sol_balance_after</code> / <code>virtual_token_balance_after</code>, so the <em>latest</em> swap per token reconstructs the current curve state for free. One query finds every standard curve stalled in the band:</p><div class="highlighted_code_block" data-attrs="{&quot;language&quot;:&quot;sql&quot;,&quot;nodeId&quot;:&quot;c72f8b78-7489-4c64-a816-db0da7c1bb4c&quot;}" data-component-name="HighlightedCodeBlockToDOM"><pre class="shiki"><code class="language-sql">SELECT
    base_coin,
    argMax(virtual_sol_balance_after,  block_time) / 1e9  AS vsol,
    argMax(virtual_token_balance_after, block_time) / 1e12 AS vtok_M,
    max(block_time) AS last_swap,
    count()         AS swaps
FROM pumpfun_all_swaps
GROUP BY base_coin

12%) — it’s <code>boost_sol / avg_boost_price</code>, so mooning tokens burn almost nothing and dumping tokens burn a lot. But <strong>holder receipt is pinned at 67.41 SOL in every single row</strong>, because that number is set by the pool’s real reserve, not by anything the burn does. The deflation is real but second-order: torching 9.5% of supply lifts each surviving token’s claim, yet the SOL that claim can be redeemed for is still capped at ~67 for the whole crowd. You can always sell — into a fixed, tiny pot, in queue order.</p><h2><strong>The honest part: +15 on paper, +2–4 in the wild</strong></h2><p>Now the reality check, because the table above is a <strong>ceiling, not an expectation.</strong></p><p>We traced completions on-chain, matching tokens in and out. A clean example — token <code>7Rauo...</code> (launched Jan 2025, genuinely dead): the completer bought <strong>486.53 M</strong> tokens for <strong>73.0 SOL</strong>, then sold the <strong>identical 486.53 M</strong> for <strong>75.7 SOL</strong>. Net <strong>+2.7 SOL</strong>. Another, <code>BxLo81...</code>: bought 410.8 M for 68.4, sold all 410.8 M for 72.55 — <strong>+4.15 SOL</strong>.</p><p>Real, repeatable, positive — but a far cry from +15. Where does the other ~11 SOL go?</p><ol><li><p><strong>The boost is a 5-minute TWAP, not a lump.</strong> You can’t dump into all 17.6 SOL instantly; it trickles, and you’re selling against the trickle.</p></li><li><p><strong>You’re not alone.</strong> The same core sniper wallets show up on nearly every one of these completions (one wallet hit 13 of 14 tokens we traced), triggered within 0–6 s of pool creation. They eat boost alongside you.</p></li><li><p><strong>Slippage is real</strong>, and the model’s <code>dump</code> is optimistic about how much the pool absorbs before the price caves.</p></li></ol><h3><strong>Stuck-bag risk — and the thing that actually kills you</strong></h3><p>Our first instinct was “you can get stuck holding an unsellable bag.” Digging in, that was <strong>half wrong</strong>, and the correction is the most important lesson here.</p><p>We initially flagged wallet <code>6QE83Z</code> as a −60 SOL disaster. It wasn’t — that was a <strong>measurement artifact</strong>: we’d summed its AMM sells inside a 6-hour window that cut off <em>before it finished unwinding</em>. Pull the full history and it sold <strong>every</strong> token, for <strong>+4 SOL</strong>, just slowly. You can almost always sell. The naive fear is wrong.</p><p>The <em>real</em> risk is subtler and it’s a competition for a finite pot. As the math above shows, the migrated pool holds only ~<strong>85 real SOL</strong> of extractable value (67.41 real + the 17.6 boost) — the virtual 17.58 is a floor nobody can withdraw. Everyone selling shares that pot. Your bag is worth ~your bag — <strong>if you’re the only seller.</strong> The dangerous competitor isn’t the snipers (they flip small); it’s the <strong>old bagholders</strong>. When a genuinely old token migrates, every wallet that bought its curve in 2024–25 and got stranded can <em>finally</em> sell into the same pool. If a large dormant float wakes up, they drain the 85 SOL alongside you and your later sells fetch dust.</p><p>Why the arb works at all: on truly dead tokens, those old holders <strong>stay asleep</strong> — they abandoned the coin months ago and aren’t watching. That dormancy <em>is</em> the edge. Which flips the target-selection criterion: you don’t just want a curve at ~66 SOL, you want one whose <strong>non-completer float is small or provably inactive</strong>. The ideal victim is a curve that stalled because everyone already left — not one with a wall of stranded holders about to get a liquidity event courtesy of your completion buy.</p><h2><strong>Takeaways</strong></h2><ul><li><p><strong>“Dead liquidity” is marketing.</strong> An AMM pool is just another constant-product curve. The ~17.58 SOL was never extractable in <em>either</em> regime — old pools locked it as real SOL (measured: 84.99 real, 0 virtual); new pools relabel it virtual, pump pockets the freed 17.58 and re-spends it as a buyback. The dead liquidity is still dead.</p></li><li><p><strong>Holders get the same deal before and after.</strong> All 793 M circulating, dumping together, extract exactly <strong>67.41 SOL</strong> (a 79% haircut) in both regimes; ~17.58 is dead either way. BOOST only changed <em>who</em> skims the freed 17.58 — arbers and snipers, not the holders it “reinjects” for.</p></li><li><p><strong>Migration never preserved </strong><code>k</code><strong>.</strong> The pool is seeded at 55% of the curve’s depth (<code>17,585</code> vs <code>32,190</code>) — true pre-BOOST too. That structural shallowness is the whole reason the circulating float can’t come home.</p></li><li><p><strong>You’re arbing a double count.</strong> During the 5-min window the same 17.58 SOL is booked as phantom virtual reserves <em>and</em> re-injected as a real buyback; the arber times the overlap to grab both before holders can.</p></li><li><p><strong>The burn is inverse to price.</strong> Same 17.58 budget torched 0.018% of supply on a mooning token and up to 12% on a dumping one — while holder receipt stayed pinned at 67.41 SOL regardless.</p></li><li><p><strong>The sweet spot is a curve at ~50–70 real SOL, stalled.</strong> Peak model EV ~+15 at S≈66; <strong>real world is +2 to +4</strong> after TWAP timing, snipers, and slippage.</p></li><li><p><strong>Indexer to find, chain to confirm.</strong> <code>vsol*vtok ≈ k</code> filters standard curves in one line; live PDA reads are mandatory because index state lags the completing swap.</p></li><li><p><strong>The kill risk is old-holder overhang</strong>, not “can’t sell.” All sellers combined can extract only ~85 real SOL; target curves whose stranded float is dormant.</p></li></ul><p>This is research tooling and market-structure analysis, not trading advice. The edge is real, small, crowded, and entirely at pump.fun’s discretion — they invented the 17.6 SOL and they can un-invent it in a single deploy.</p><div><hr></div><p><em>Analysis code lives in our research repo at </em><code>experiments/pumpfun_boost_arb/</code><em>.
