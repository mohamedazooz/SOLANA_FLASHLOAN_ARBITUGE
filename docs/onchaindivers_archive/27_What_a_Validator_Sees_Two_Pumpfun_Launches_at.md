# What a Validator Sees: Two Pumpfun Launches at Millisecond Resolution

- **Date**: 2026-06-10
- **Original URL**: [https://onchaindivers.substack.com/p/what-a-validator-sees-two-pumpfun](https://onchaindivers.substack.com/p/what-a-validator-sees-two-pumpfun)
- **Description**: When a token launches on Pump.fun, the contention happens in well under a second.

---

<p>When a token launches on Pump.fun, the contention happens in well under a second. Block explorers can’t show this — they stop at slot granularity (~400 ms) and slot timestamps are usually second-precision. This post zooms in on what a <em>single Solana validator</em> actually saw during two real Pump.fun launches that landed in its blocks.</p><p>We use one validator — <code>EvnRmnMrd69kFdbLMxWkTn1icZ7DCceRhvmb2SJXqDo4</code> — and look only at swap activity that occurred in its <strong>own</strong> post-launch slots. Solana validators get up to 4 consecutive slots per leader rotation, so when a token launches in the first slot of such a rotation, this validator effectively <em>owns</em> the whole launch window.</p><h2><strong>How to read the charts</strong></h2><p>Every bar is <strong>one swap attempt</strong> against the new token’s Pump.fun bonding curve, observed in a slot produced by this validator. That’s the whole point: most pumpfun-launch visualizations smear this away.</p><p>The fee shown is the <strong>realized lamports the signer was charged</strong>, regardless of outcome. Failed transactions still pay their priority fee, and that’s the most informative signal for “who was willing to burn SOL to be first.”</p><h2><strong>Methodology — restricting to one validator’s slots</strong></h2><p>We do <strong>not</strong> count swaps that happened in other validators’ slots. The procedure is:</p><ol><li><p>List every slot produced by <code>EvnRm…XqDo4</code> in the last 72 h (11,349 blocks).</p></li><li><p>Group those slots into <em>leader runs</em> — maximal contiguous sequences of slots (Solana gives each leader up to 4 in a row).</p></li><li><p>Find every pumpfun token whose creation transaction landed in one of those slots.</p></li><li><p>For each launch in slot <code>S</code> with run-end <code>R</code>, count swaps in slots <code>S, S+1, …, R</code> (all owned by this validator).</p></li><li><p>Rank by trade count, take the top two.</p></li></ol><p>The two winners both landed in the <strong>first slot</strong> of a 4-slot leader run, so the validator produced the entire post-launch window.</p><h2><strong>Launch #1 — Muttley Crew The geyser <code>entry_timestamp</code> resolves that. Scoping the window to a single validator’s slots tells you what <em>that validator alone</em> witnessed — which is the right frame for asking “did the validator give anyone an advantage?” and “did snipers burn fees on this validator vs another?”</p><p>The visual takeaway: most of a validator’s 1-second leader window during a hot launch is <strong>empty time</strong>. The fight is two or three 50–100 ms bursts. The rest is decoration. Lowering latency-to-leader matters far more than raising the priority fee — both winning bidders here paid less than the average loser.</p><h2><strong>Technical details</strong></h2><p><strong>Data sources</strong></p><ul><li><p><code>solana_blocks</code> — to identify slots produced by validator <code>EvnRm…XqDo4</code> and group them into leader runs (consecutive slots same validator).</p></li><li><p><code>pumpfun_token_creation</code> — for the mint and launch slot. Only launches whose creation slot belongs to one of this validator’s runs are considered.</p></li><li><p><code>pumpfun_v2_swaps</code> — every buy/sell attempt against the new token’s bonding curve, with success/failure status and realized <code>fee</code>. Restricted to slots <code>[launch_slot, run_end]</code>.</p></li><li><p><code>tx_timestamps</code> — geyser per-tx <code>entry_timestamp</code> (unix seconds with fractional part) used as the x-axis.</p></li></ul><p><strong>Two caveats we hit</strong></p><ul><li><p><code>solana_blocks</code> sometimes contains duplicate rows for the same <code>(slot, validator)</code> — we deduplicate with <code>SELECT DISTINCT slot</code> before grouping into leader runs.</p></li><li><p><code>pumpfun_v2_swaps</code> can have multiple rows per <code>(slot, tx_idx, signature)</code> (one per swap instruction within the same transaction). We collapse them with <code>GROUP BY slot, tx_idx … any(*)</code> so each transaction contributes exactly one bar.</p></li></ul><p><strong>Interactive viewer</strong> A live version where you can paste any validator address and render the top 3 launches in this format is bundled with this research as the <code>pumpfun-launch-timeline</code> app — paste a validator pubkey and you get the same chart for any leader.</p><p><strong>Reproducibility</strong></p><pre><code><code>uv run experiments/validator_launch_timeline/01_find_launches.py
