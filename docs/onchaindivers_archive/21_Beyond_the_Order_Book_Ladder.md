Those two statements look similar in an ordinary L2 view—each is simply a price and a size—but their underlying composition is very different.</p><p>The recovered feed also makes lifecycle analysis possible. A specific order can be tied to its wallet and oid, then followed through an update to its remaining size, a fill, and, where a status event is available, a cancellation. Creation timestamps remain attached to resting orders, including orders created long before the selected analysis interval.</p><p>There is an important boundary: the data does <strong>not</strong> publish an explicit matching-engine queue rank. <strong>Creation timestamps and order IDs</strong> support chronological analysis, but they do not independently prove strict FIFO priority within a price level. And because book diffs, statuses, and fills are exported as separate streams, a full replay should merge them by block time rather than rely on file-line order alone.</p><p>For Polymarket, the same service exposes a different layer: captured L2 books and level changes. That is enough to study spread, displayed depth, and liquidity movement, but it does not reveal individual order IDs, wallets, creation times, or queue position.</p><p>That distinction matters. The goal is not to manufacture alpha from a picture of a book.
