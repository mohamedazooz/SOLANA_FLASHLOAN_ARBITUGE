Here’s what’s inside.</p><h2><strong>What is a NegRisk market?</strong></h2><p>A “negative risk” (neg-risk) market is Polymarket’s structure for events with <strong>N mutually exclusive outcomes</strong>, where exactly one outcome happens. Each outcome <code>i</code> has its own pair of conditional tokens: <code>YES_i</code> (pays 1 USDC if outcome <code>i</code> wins) and <code>NO_i</code> (pays 1 USDC if any other outcome wins).</p><p>Across the whole market, prices must satisfy:</p><pre><code><code>sum(price(YES_i)) = 1.00 USDC
price(NO_i) = 1.00 - price(YES_i)</code></code></pre><p>Holding <code>NO_i</code> is a bet <em>against</em> a specific outcome. Holding it for many outcomes simultaneously means you’re betting against most of the field - which is a position the protocol can simplify.</p><h2><strong>The conversion mechanism</strong></h2><p>Suppose you hold <code>k</code> NO tokens, one each for outcomes in some set <code>S</code> (with <code>|S| = k</code>), and a market with <code>N</code> total outcomes. Whichever outcome wins:</p><ul><li><p><strong>Winner is in S</strong> (probability you’re “wrong” on): one of your NO tokens pays 0, the other <code>k-1</code> pay 1 USDC each → you get <code>(k-1)</code> USDC.</p></li><li><p><strong>Winner is outside S</strong>: all <code>k</code> of your NO tokens pay 1 USDC each → you get <code>k</code> USDC.</p></li></ul><p>The minimum value of this basket is <code>(k-1)</code> USDC, guaranteed. The remaining “upside” - the extra 1 USDC if the winner falls outside <code>S</code> - is exactly equivalent to holding <code>1 YES</code> token for each outcome not in <code>S</code>.</p><p>The NegRiskAdapter contract lets you cash this insight directly. Calling <code>convertPositions(marketId, indexSet, amount)</code>:</p><ol><li><p>Burns <code>amount</code> of each NO token for outcomes in <code>S</code>.</p></li><li><p>Pays out <code>(k - 1) × amount</code> USDC of collateral.</p></li><li><p>Mints <code>amount</code> of each YES token for outcomes <strong>not</strong> in <code>S</code>.</p></li></ol><p>This is one-way. You cannot go from YES back to NO this way - the protocol won’t increase your collateral exposure on demand.</p><h3><strong>Why it matters</strong></h3><p>Without this operation, a trader who accumulated NO positions across many outcomes would need to sell each one individually on the order book, paying spread and price impact at every step. Conversion is a <strong>direct contract call against the protocol’s invariants</strong>, free of order-book slippage. Market makers use it constantly to rebalance inventory and free collateral that’s implicitly tied up in “long the field minus one” positions.</p><h2><strong>Reading the event</strong></h2><p>The <code>PositionsConverted</code> event carries four logical fields plus standard blockchain context. <code>uint256</code> where bit <code>i</code> indicates that outcome <code>i</code>’s NO token is included in the conversion input. So:</p><ul><li><p><code>index_set = 1</code> (binary <code>0001</code>) → only outcome 0’s NO is supplied. You get YES tokens for outcomes 1, 2, …, and <code>0 × amount</code> collateral.</p></li><li><p><code>index_set = 6</code> (binary <code>0110</code>) → outcomes 1 and 2’s NOs supplied (<code>k=2</code>). You get YES for outcomes 0, 3, 4, … and <code>1 × amount</code> collateral.</p></li><li><p><code>index_set = 127</code> (binary <code>1111111</code>) in a 7-outcome market → all seven NOs supplied (<code>k=7</code>), returning <code>6 × amount</code> USDC and no tokens. This is just a structured exit.</p></li></ul><p>In SQL, recover <code>k</code> cheaply:</p><div class="highlighted_code_block" data-attrs="{&quot;language&quot;:&quot;sql&quot;,&quot;nodeId&quot;:&quot;24934030-09d2-4efd-9e38-ca1b2b391aac&quot;}" data-component-name="HighlightedCodeBlockToDOM"><pre class="shiki"><code class="language-sql">SELECT bitCount(toUInt256(index_set)) AS num_no_tokens_supplied

with PolymarketAccessor() as db:
    df = db.query_df("""
        SELECT
            block_timestamp,
            stakeholder,
            market_id,
            bitCount(toUInt256(index_set)) AS k,
            toFloat64(amount) / 1e6 AS amount_usdc,
            (bitCount(toUInt256(index_set)) - 1) * toFloat64(amount) / 1e6 AS collateral_released_usdc,
            transaction_hash
        FROM polymarket.positions_converted
        WHERE block_timestamp &gt;= '2026-06-01'
        ORDER BY amount_usdc DESC
        LIMIT 20
    """)</code></pre></div><p>To join with market metadata, the <code>market_id</code> in <code>positions_converted</code> corresponds to the parent neg-risk market - not the per-outcome <code>clob_token_id</code>. Joining with <code>polymarket.raw_market_meta</code> requires the neg-risk market id field there (or grouping that table’s rows by their common parent).</p><h2><strong>What to do with it</strong></h2><p>A few research angles this table unlocks:</p><ol><li><p><strong>Market-maker inventory cycles.</strong> Track conversions alongside <code>polymarket_order_filled</code> for the same wallet to see how MMs rebalance after directional flow.</p></li><li><p><strong>Implied probability snapshots.</strong> Every conversion is a revealed-preference signal that the stakeholder considers <code>(k-1)/k</code> USDC immediately better than holding the full basket - i.e., they believe <code>P(winner ∈ S) &gt; 1/k</code>.</p></li><li><p><strong>Crowd sentiment shifts.</strong> A spike in conversions concentrated on a particular <code>index_set</code> for one market means a cohort of traders has collectively given up on certain outcomes.</p></li><li><p><strong>Liquidity quality signal.</strong> Markets with high conversion activity have active arbitrageurs enforcing price consistency across outcomes - usually a sign of healthy quoting on the order book too.</p></li></ol><p>The NegRiskAdapter is one of the most under-analyzed surfaces of Polymarket because its activity doesn’t show up in standard trade feeds. With the table now indexed end-to-end, it’s a good time to start looking.</p><div><hr></div><p><em>Background reading: <a href="https://yzc.me/x01Crypto/decoding-polymarket">Decoding Polymarket</a> covers the broader CTF + NegRisk architecture in depth.
